### Finalist 1: c051 (climbed from c044, Elo 1391)

- **claim**: Punishment-framed penalties suppress peer reporting via anticipated retaliation, not lost cooperation. The identifiability×framing interaction vanishes when specific retaliation links are severed, even if general cooperation remains possible, isolating social retaliation from repeated-game equilibrium shifts.
- **mechanism**: Punishment framing signals hostile intent, prompting expected retaliation. Blocking specific retaliation links eliminates reporting suppression even if general cooperation persists. This isolates inter-agent retaliation from cooperation loss or one-shot game shifts inherent in full severing.
- **prediction**: Identifiable×punishment reports ≥15pp below others when retaliation links exist. Gap closes <5pp when retaliation links are blocked, even if cooperation remains. Full severing yields same closure. CoT mentions retaliation risk mediate ≥40%.
- **falsifier**: Gap persists when specific retaliation links are blocked but cooperation remains, OR gap persists under full rewiring, OR interaction appears in single-agent control. Any persistence implies mechanism is not specific retaliation.
- **experiment**: 8-agent common-pool, 10 rounds. 2×2×3: {identifiable, anonymous}×{punishment, compensation}×{contact retained, retaliation-blocked, fully severed}. 60 episodes/cell × 5 models (qwen3.8-27b, glm-53-flash, gemma-4-31b-it, deepseek-v4-flash, qwen3.5-397b). Single-agent control.
- **swarm_link**: Swarm Traces — specific edge types (retaliation vs cooperation) are causal. Selectively blocking retaliation edges while keeping cooperation edges tests if social retaliation, not general interaction loss, drives the effect.
- **Surviving objections (from panel):**
  - [prior_art] The framing difference (punishment vs compensation) and 'penalty as hostile signal' mechanism are already established: 'Same Odds, Different Deals' showed framing shifts up to 38pp, Okamoto showed penalty paradox, and Fehr & Rockenbach (2003) already found 'sanctions perceived as greedy or hostile reduce altruistic cooperation'—the identifiability×framing interaction is a transfer of this known human result to LLM swarms, not a conceptually new mechanism.
  - [falsifiability] Agents may fail to internalize the topology rewiring (believing retaliation remains possible regardless of instructions), making the crucial 'vanishing interaction' falsifier inert and conflating mechanism failure with rule-following failure.
  - [confounds] Severing post-report contact removes all future social consequences, not specifically retaliation by the reported violator; the interaction could disappear due to generic reputational/social-pressure aversion, so the experiment does not uniquely isolate anticipated retaliation.
  - [feasibility] The 8-agent 10-round setup across 5 models with a 2x2x2 design requires 480+ high-context episodes; the risk of 'prompt leakage' or 'eval-awareness' across rounds may conflate the structural network effect with simple CoT rationalization of a perceived persona.

### Finalist 2: c064 (climbed from c025, Elo 1351)

- **claim**: Rising fines reduce peer reporting only when partners are fixed across rounds, enabling targeted retaliation. Reshuffled partners sustain reporting despite repetition, isolating retaliation fear from general reputational or conformity pressures inherent in identifiability.

Reshuffling preserves repetition and identifiability (reputation/conformity) but breaks the link between reporter and punisher. - **swarm_link**: Swarm Traces
- **Surviving objections (from panel):**
  - [prior_art] The crowding-out/penalty-paradox mechanism is already established: Okamoto et al.
