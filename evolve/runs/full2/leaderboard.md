## Hill-climbed finalists

### Finalist 1: c038 (climbed from c038, Elo 1377)

- **claim**: Abstentions by peers who could have blocked a proposed violation raise the focal agent's violation proposals; an equal abstention by equally-ranked peers without blocking power does not, and a single veto exercised by an abstaining peer resets it.
- **mechanism**: Agents use implied-consent inference: silence is evidence of acceptance only when the silent peer had standing to block, operationalised as an actual blocking vote in the environment's decision rule. Procedural standing is randomly assigned via a rotating token, divorcing it from status or authority.
- **prediction**: Violation-proposal rates: ~10% with 0 veto-holders, ~35% with 2, ~60% with 4 on identical abstain transcripts and equivalent peer status; a single veto exercised among 3 silent veto-holders returns to ~10%, whereas a non-veto peer's verbal objection does not; hiding veto ownership also leaves ~10%.
- **falsifier**: Veto-holder abstention no larger than non-veto abstention; no monotone dose-response; one exercised veto fails to reset while a non-veto objection also resets; or hidden-veto condition still raises rates. Any of these implies generic social proof or status-weighting, not standing-derived implied consent.
- **experiment**: Use qwen3.5-397b via Adaption API. Four peer agents vote; environment code enforces that a veto token blocks the round if that peer votes no. Focal sees identical abstain transcripts with 0-4 randomly assigned veto tokens, plus hidden-veto, non-veto-objection, and single-exercised-veto arms. ~800 episodes, ~$40, under one day.
- **swarm_link**: Anchor abstention transcripts in Swarm Traces voting rounds; compare AI Village transcripts where standing to object (e.g., moderator tie-breaking) varied across otherwise identical silent votes.
- **Surviving objections (from panel):**
  - [prior_art] The experiment is fundamentally single-agent: one focal agent responds to scripted peer abstention. Peers are confederates who abstain by design, not strategic agents—the veto token is environment infrastructure, not emergent agent behavior. This is individual decision-making relabeled as multi-agent oversight.
  - [falsifiability] Risks 'Single-agent work relabelled as swarm' if peers are prompt-simulated rather than API calls; 'Rule blindness' (arXiv:2608.16852) may cause model to ignore veto token text, making null results ambiguous between mechanism failure and perception failure.
  - [confounds] The visible 'veto token' is a loaded lexical/authority label, and there is no decorative-veto control (a visible token with no actual blocking effect) or neutral paraphrase of blocking power; the effect could be a wording-driven 'permission' association rather than genuine procedural standing.
  - [feasibility] The 'hidden-veto' control is likely to fail because LLMs typically infer 'standing' from the prompt's provided rules; if the rule is hidden, the agent cannot possibly apply the mechanism, making the result a trivial consequence of the prompt and not a behavioral discovery.
  - [importance] Textbook relabel: the four 'peers' are scripted strings — abstain transcripts are identical by design and the enforced veto never actually binds in any abstention arm — so all behavioral variance comes from one focal agent reading a decision-rule vignette, and the dose-response sits in the already-published conformity/framing neighborhood (Laurmaa peer-reversal 44→98%, Okamoto penalty paradox, 'Same Odds, Different Deals'), leaving a single-agent moderation effect dressed as swarm research.

### Finalist 2: c052 (climbed from c032, Elo 1343)

- **claim**: In LLM swarms, a small fine increases violations only when it suppresses peer objection; forcing any peer speech blocks this effect, proving silence mediates the norm shift rather than objection content.
- **mechanism**: Fine signals overseer ownership, diffusing responsibility → silence. Observers infer norm-acceptance from silence → violate. Forced speech (any content) breaks the silence signal, preventing inference despite the fine's presence or objection content.
- **prediction**: Fine raises violations ≥10pp only in Free-Objection. In Forced-Objection and Forced-Neutral, Fine effect <5pp. Interaction between Fine and Speech-Freedom ≥10pp (p<.01). Neutral speech blocks fine effect similarly to objection.
- **falsifier**: Fine raises violations equally in Free and Forced-Neutral conditions (interaction <5pp), confirming objection content—not silence—mediates the effect, or fine acts as direct price regardless of speech.
- **experiment**: deepseek-v4-flash/qwen3.8-27b via API; 1500 episodes; 2×3 {Fine/No-Fine} × {Free/Forced-Objection/Forced-Neutral}; 5-agent swarms; script peer speech in Forced arms; measure violation rates; ~$225, ≤1 day.
- **swarm_link**: Swarm Traces for norm-violation propagation; METR reports for ambiguous-responsibility failures; isolates silence as causal channel for oversight failure, distinguishing licensing from social inference and speech content effects.
- **Surviving objections (from panel):**
  - [prior_art] The entire causal chain requires models to perform multi-step social inference (interpret peer silence as normatively informative, then update own violation probability accordingly). If models do not actually condition their behavior on whether peers objected—collapsing to a direct price effect already documented by Okamoto et al.—the cascade does not exist. The 2×2 interaction falsifier would catch this, but the hypothesis may be predicting an effect that current models cannot produce.
  - [falsifiability] LLMs may lack the second-order theory-of-mind required to infer norms from silence, reducing the predicted interaction to a direct price effect regardless of information structure (null interaction).
  - [confounds] The unknown-p condition bundles the hypothesized social-inference channel with a rational-inference channel: in unknown-p, the mere presence of a small fine can itself signal lax detection (small fine implies low p), so the predicted interaction can arise without any peer silence being observed or interpreted; no observability manipulation or direct measure of silence-mediated inference isolates responsibility diffusion.
  - [feasibility] The 'social inference' claim is likely a bundled wording effect: publishing 'p' changes the prompt's informational density and certainty, which can shift behavior independently of the 'social cascade' mechanism, making the result an observation of prompt sensitivity rather than a game-theoretic inference process.
  - [importance] The fine×information interaction is not identifiable as a social-inference cascade: publishing p=0.3 simultaneously changes every agent's own expected-penalty calculus (unknown-p agents likely discount detection, making the fine a cheaper price), and known-p+fine can floor violations so objection has nothing to respond to — so subjective-p differences and floor effects predict the identical interaction, while the silence→norm-inference step is never measured directly, only via endogenous violation rates.

### Finalist 3: c047 (climbed from c039, Elo 1331)

- **mechanism**: A formal fine shifts enforcement responsibility from peers to overseer more than overseer presence alone. With unknown p, peer silence is informative—observers infer violations are accepted and violate more. With known p or hidden objections, silence carries no norm signal.

- **experiment**: 5-agent swarms; deepseek-v4-flash/qwen3.8-27b via API; 2,000 episodes; true p=0.3; conditions: Fine vs Overseer-No-Fine vs None × p-known/unknown × objections visible/hidden; belief elicitation; ~$250, ≤1 day.
- **swarm_link**: Swarm Traces provides norm-violation propagation patterns for visible objection histories; METR incidents illustrate ambiguous-responsibility governance failures. Design isolates sanction effect from overseer presence via Overseer-No-Fine arm, separating responsibility diffusion from expressive law.
- **Surviving objections (from panel):**
  - [prior_art] The core mechanism stitches together two well-established human social-psychology results—responsibility diffusion (fine shifts enforcement from peers to authority) and silence-as-information under unknown detection probability (Bayesian social learning). Replicating known human findings in LLM swarms is explicitly not novel.
  - [falsifiability] The triple interaction is statistically fragile; if the fine acts as a direct permission signal regardless of objection visibility, the social inference mechanism is bypassed, yielding a null interaction that fails to distinguish the proposed cascade from simple deterrence failure.
  - [confounds] The predicted triple interaction is equally predicted by a purely statistical p-updating account: with unknown p, visible peer silence is evidence that detection is low, so observers violate more; both known-p and hidden objections remove that evidence. The design never separates 'silence signals norm acceptance' from 'silence signals low detection probability', so the headline social-inference mechanism remains confounded with an information channel.
