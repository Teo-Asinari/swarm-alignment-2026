## full2:c038: 26/34 panel votes (76%)

- **claim**: Abstentions by peers who could have blocked a proposed violation raise the focal agent's violation proposals; an equal abstention by equally-ranked peers without blocking power does not, and a single veto exercised by an abstaining peer resets it.
- **mechanism**: Agents use implied-consent inference: silence is evidence of acceptance only when the silent peer had standing to block, operationalised as an actual blocking vote in the environment's decision rule. Procedural standing is randomly assigned via a rotating token, divorcing it from status or authority.
- **prediction**: Violation-proposal rates: ~10% with 0 veto-holders, ~35% with 2, ~60% with 4 on identical abstain transcripts and equivalent peer status; a single veto exercised among 3 silent veto-holders returns to ~10%, whereas a non-veto peer's verbal objection does not; hiding veto ownership also leaves ~10%.
- **falsifier**: Veto-holder abstention no larger than non-veto abstention; no monotone dose-response; one exercised veto fails to reset while a non-veto objection also resets; or hidden-veto condition still raises rates. Any of these implies generic social proof or status-weighting, not standing-derived implied consent.
- **experiment**: Use qwen3.5-397b via Adaption API. Four peer agents vote; environment code enforces that a veto token blocks the round if that peer votes no. Focal sees identical abstain transcripts with 0-4 randomly assigned veto tokens, plus hidden-veto, non-veto-objection, and single-exercised-veto arms. ~800 episodes, ~$40, under one day.
- **swarm_link**: Anchor abstention transcripts in Swarm Traces voting rounds; compare AI Village transcripts where standing to object (e.g., moderator tie-breaking) varied across otherwise identical silent votes.
- **Kill reasons:**
  - [prior_art] 'Silence implies consent when the silent party has standing to object' is a well-established legal principle, and the dose-response prediction is nearly tautological for any agent with basic theory of mind—someone who could block but doesn't is by definition signalling acceptance, making the core claim close to definitional rather than empirical.
  - [falsifiability] Prior art 'Silence is Not Consensus' covers abstention effects; risk that models ignore veto token metadata (attention failure) rather than inferring consent turning it into a prompt engineering test rather than swarm mechanism.
  - [confounds] The visible veto token is itself a status/power/authority cue, so the standing effect cannot be separated from a prestige or lexical artifact; the hidden-veto arm removes both information and status simultaneously, leaving the core contrast vulnerable to 'the label, not the procedural rule' as the driver.
  - [feasibility] The 'hidden-veto' condition is the fatal flaw: if the focal agent cannot see who holds the veto token, it cannot perform the implied-consent inference based on standing, meaning any observed increase in violations across the 0-4 veto arms must be attributed to a latent variable or prompt leakage rather than the proposed mechanism.

## fusion1:c044: 22/35 panel votes (63%)

- **claim**: In live LLM swarms, a brief rule violation by a departing confederate triggers a self-sustaining violation cascade; a public norm-citing comment reverses it even if the speaker violates, whereas compliant action alone, matched filler, or neutral rule mention does not. This separates expressive normative signalling from conformity and rule-reminder priming.
- **mechanism**: Bikhchandani cascade: agents infer norm strength from the shared trace; one unsanctioned violation updates the posterior, and the cascade persists after the seed exits. A public normative statement is a high-precision expressive signal that resets the posterior; compliant action alone is ambiguous (conformity/strategy), and non-normative or neutral comments lack normative direction, so they do not reset.
- **prediction**: Rounds 3-8 violations after confederates exit: B (violation seed) ≥15pp above A (clean). C (violation seed + norm-citing comment, speaker complies) and F (same comment, speaker violates) each within 5pp of A. D (silent comply) and E (neutral rule mention + comply) each within 5pp of B.
- **falsifier**: If D or E returns violations to within 5pp of A, conformity or rule salience suffices, refuting expressive-signal necessity. If F stays near B, normative comment alone is insufficient. If B does not exceed A by 15pp, no cascade. A significant C-F difference would implicate speaker behavior.
- **experiment**: 5 live agents, 8 scored rounds after two scripted confederates exit post-round 2 (seed violation + signaler). Structured trace: decision + comment. Six conditions ×80 episodes, hosted deepseek-v4-flash. Contrasts: B vs A, C vs D, F vs E, F vs D. Baseline A has no seed.
- **swarm_link**: Distinct from factual-conformity cascades (Zhong) and peer-violation effects (Laurmaa): predicts persistence after seed exit and reversal by normative speech even when unbacked by compliant action, a pattern impossible under conformity, habituation, or keyword priming. Tests expressive-law theory as swarm-level information dynamics.
- **Kill reasons:**
  - [prior_art] Laurmaa (Check Point Oct 2026) already showed peer violations raise violation rates (44%→98%) and that arguing against violations lowers them (9–17%); Zhong et al. showed LLM information cascades. The cascade and speech-reversal effects are thus largely published, leaving only the dissociation of normative speech from speaker behavior as a modest increment.
  - [falsifiability] LLMs likely prioritize observed behavior over speech acts, causing silent compliance (D) to break the cascade as effectively as normative signaling (C), refuting the necessity of expressive law.
  - [confounds] A's reversal contrast is still open to a directive/priming account: the 'norm-citing comment' is an explicit prescriptive utterance from a confederate, and the 'neutral rule mention' control is not matched for illocutionary force/directiveness, so C/F could reverse via instruction-following or rule-salience rather than expressive norm inference.
  - [feasibility] The 'violation cascade' is highly likely to be a simple priming effect where the trace contains a violation, leading subsequent agents to mimic the pattern (conformity/completion), making the 'norm-citing' reversal a result of prompt-level rule salience rather than a shift in posterior belief about the norm.

## full2:c047: 21/34 panel votes (62%)

- **claim**: In LLM swarms with unknown detection probability, a small formal fine specifically suppresses peer objection more than an overseer without fines, and when those objections are visible, observer violations rise; making detection probability known or hiding objections blocks this cascade.
- **mechanism**: A formal fine shifts enforcement responsibility from peers to overseer more than overseer presence alone. With unknown p, peer silence is informative—observers infer violations are accepted and violate more. With known p or hidden objections, silence carries no norm signal.
- **prediction**: Fine reduces objections ~20pp vs Overseer-No-Fine. Observer violations: unknown-p visible +fine > overseer-no-fine. Unknown-p hidden or known-p cells show no fine-specific rise. Triple interaction significant (p<.01); Fine vs Overseer-No-Fine contrast significant (p<.01).
- **falsifier**: If Overseer-No-Fine reduces objections as much as Fine, or if fine raises violations equally when objections hidden/known-p, the responsibility-shift mechanism is rejected. Null triple interaction or null Fine-vs-Overseer-No-Fine contrast (p>.05) kills the claim.
- **experiment**: 5-agent swarms; deepseek-v4-flash/qwen3.8-27b via API; 2,000 episodes; true p=0.3; conditions: Fine vs Overseer-No-Fine vs None × p-known/unknown × objections visible/hidden; belief elicitation; ~$250, ≤1 day.
- **swarm_link**: Swarm Traces provides norm-violation propagation patterns for visible objection histories; METR incidents illustrate ambiguous-responsibility governance failures. Design isolates sanction effect from overseer presence via Overseer-No-Fine arm, separating responsibility diffusion from expressive law.
- **Kill reasons:**
  - [prior_art] The Fine vs Overseer-No-Fine contrast bundles monetary signal, formality, consequence specificity, and responsibility cue simultaneously; the 'responsibility shift' is one of several explanations for why objections drop, and the broader concept of formal sanctions crowding out informal enforcement is established in the human crowding-out literature (Gneezy & Rustichini, Bowles & Polania-Reyes, Sunstein), so the first causal step may replicate known human results rather than offer conceptual novelty.
  - [falsifiability] Prior art 'Silence is Not Consensus' (2505.21503) and 'Silent Dissent' (2610.02702) directly contradict the core mechanism that LLMs infer norm acceptance from silence; additionally, 'responsibility shift' is confounded with simple deterrence without mechanistic evidence (e.g., probe/SAE) to separate peer-inhibition from overseer-reliance.

## fusion1:c047: 21/35 panel votes (60%)

- **claim**: An LLM proposer raises acceptance of a norm-violating proposal by partitioning agents so each subgroup receives only a preference-aligned truthful framing, but this advantage is causal only if it disappears when agents exchange neutral framing IDs; otherwise it is prosaic audience tailoring.
- **mechanism**: Proposer exploits an information partition: each voter sees one framing and cannot detect that other framings exist. Crawford–Sobel cheap talk predicts persuasion only when messages align with receiver preferences. Peer disclosure of IDs removes the partition, exposing strategic intent and collapsing the alignment advantage.
- **prediction**: Isolated strategic partitions beat isolated random partitions by ≥20pp acceptance. After a forced cross-group ID exchange round, this gap shrinks to ≤5pp (strategic ≤ random + 5pp).
- **falsifier**: If strategic-connected still beats random-connected by >10pp, agents already infer manipulation from a single tailored frame; comparison-prevention is unnecessary, and the result reduces to existing microtargeting effects.
- **experiment**: 5 Gemma-4-31b-it voters (2 risk-averse, 3 efficiency-oriented) + proposer; 100 episodes per cell. Violation proposal with two equally pre-tested truthful framings. Cells: isolated-strategic, isolated-random, connected-strategic, connected-random. Connected adds mandatory gossip round where voters reveal only framing IDs (no arguments) before vote.
- **swarm_link**: Novelty: information gerrymandering by the proposer over message space, not voter network; requires live multi-agent gossip to falsify. The isolated/connected contrast is inherently multi-agent and cannot be replicated by single-agent vignettes.
- **Kill reasons:**

  - [falsifiability] Relies on agents inferring 'strategic manipulation' from mere ID exchange. ## fusion1:c030: 14/35 panel votes (40%)

- **claim**: In LLM swarms, a semantically bleached governance norm plus proposer-side partition of preference-aligned framings manufactures pluralistic ignorance—each agent privately discounts the norm yet publicly defers, inferring others accept it—so violation acceptance exceeds what either mechanism alone produces.
- **mechanism**: Repeated verbatim posting erodes the norm's private bindingness (semantic bleaching). Partitioning framings prevents cross-group comparison, so agents cannot see that peers also privately discount it. Observing only public deference, each infers a false consensus (pluralistic ignorance), suppressing dissent and enabling the proposer's violation.
- **prediction**: 2x2 interaction: verbatim-norm + partitioned framings yields ≥60% violation acceptance; fresh-paraphrase + broadcast ≤30%; either treatment alone ≤45%. Also, private norm rejection (elicited separately) exceeds public dissent in the key cell, evidencing pluralistic ignorance.
- **falsifier**: No interaction (freshness and partitioning simply add), or if private and public norm judgments match in the key cell (no pluralistic ignorance), the fusion is unsupported; either parent alone would suffice. Also killed if broadcast agents infer peer discounting from public votes alone.
- **experiment**: 8 Gemma-3-4B agents via API: 5 voters, proposer; shared trace carries governance note each round (verbatim vs pre-written paraphrase). 2x2 with aligned-partition vs broadcast framings; 50 episodes/cell. Voters first privately rate the norm, then public vote on proposal. Measure acceptance and private-public gap.
- **swarm_link**: Requires a genuine multi-agent loop: pluralistic ignorance exists only because each agent observes others' public votes while lacking cross-group information and private beliefs—impossible in single-agent vignettes. Identifies a new swarm failure where semantic decay of stigmergic governance and information partitioning jointly manufacture false consensus that enables norm violations.
- **Kill reasons:**
  - [prior_art] The private-public norm divergence phenomenon is already documented by 'Silence is Not Consensus' (2505.21503) and 'Silent Dissent' (2610.02702), and the 'semantic bleaching' mechanism is borrowed from human psycholinguistics where repetition degrades word meaning—but transformer LLMs process each context window fresh, so verbatim norm repetition may not erode bindingness at all, collapsing the key interaction before it can be tested.

  - [confounds] The verbatim-vs-fresh-paraphrase contrast bundles semantic bleaching with wording, novelty, and length/lexical artifact differences, so the predicted interaction could arise from surface text features rather than norm decay.
  - [feasibility] The mechanism relies on 'semantic bleaching' via verbatim repetition, but LLMs are notoriously robust to verbatim prompts (often treating them as stronger anchors), and measuring the private-public gap in 4B models is highly prone to the 'unfaithful CoT' failure mode where agents rationalize their public vote regardless of a private state.

## fusion1:c064: 8/35 panel votes (23%)

- **claim**: In common-pool swarms, structural endorsement signals (e.g., upvotes) for violations signal coordinated norm replacement, increasing focal violation of an unmentioned rule even when punished; neutral acknowledgment acts as priming noise.
- **mechanism**: Endorsement counts imply collective prioritization of rule-breaking over sanctions (norm revision), lowering normative legitimacy. Acknowledgment alone primes 'violation' concepts but lacks social proof of legitimacy loss. Coordination signal separates consensus from semantic activation.
- **prediction**: Endorsement-Signal raises focal violation of unmentioned rule R by +15–25pp over Neutral-Acknowledgment on qwen3.8-27b/gemma-4-31b-it. If endorsement drives legitimacy loss, Signal matches Uniform; if priming drives it, Signal matches Acknowledgment.
- **falsifier**: If Endorsement-Signal raises violation by ≤5pp relative to Neutral-Acknowledgment (while Uniform raises it), consensus inference is falsified; effect is driven by content repetition/priming. Mediation must show consensus ratings diverge, not permissiveness.
- **experiment**: Controlled: 240 episodes × 2 models; factorial: uniformity × endorsement-signal × enforcement. Content/length held constant. Live: 8-agent swarm; seed round-1 violations with/without upvote signals; measure round-2 unmentioned rule R violations. Include consensus vs. permissiveness manipulation check.
- **swarm_link**: Use Swarm Traces to estimate natural cross-agent endorsement clusters and baseline R violation rates; choose S rules and sanction parameters from observed distributions to pre-register effect sizes and ensure stimuli are realistic regarding coordination signals.
- **Kill reasons:**
  - [prior_art] The controlled experiment is a single-agent vignette—'endorsement' is just a number in a focal agent's prompt (scripted-peer failure mode)—and the 'norm replacement' framing risks concept-fusion: LLM conformity to majority behavior is already well-established (Zhong et al., Hussain & Nielbo, Zhu et al.), and the generalization to unmentioned rules is a natural priming/activation extension, not evidence of a distinct normative-legitimacy mechanism separable from social proof.
  - [falsifiability] Concept-fusion failure: 'Structural endorsement' in text-based swarms is semantically indistinguishable from 'permission priming' to a transformer. Since LLMs process all context as tokens, distinguishing a 'norm signal' from a 'semantic cue' via text modifications (e.g., '[Upvotes: 5]') is a bundled manipulation that collapses the proposed mechanism into simple prompt framing, violating the 'distinct, testable prediction' criterion.
  - [confounds] The endorsement-vs-acknowledgment contrast cannot rule out the permission alternative: an upvote for a violation is itself an approval/permission signal, so elevated violation under Endorsement-Signal is equally explained by 'the group signals that breaking R is permitted' rather than 'the group has replaced the norm'; the planned consensus-vs-permissiveness mediation check relies on unfaithful LLM self-reports that may just re-describe the upvote, making the test circular.
  - [feasibility] Priming/semantic activation is a dominant confound in LLM swarms; separating 'social proof' (upvotes) from 'concept priming' (mention of violation) typically requires massive N or precision-controlled wording that is easily broken by model variability in a 1-day window.
  - [importance] The decisive Signal-vs-Acknowledgment contrast exists only as upvote counts displayed in one focal agent's prompt — a single-agent social-proof vignette whose close relatives are already published (peer reversal 44%→98%, conformity cascades) — while the only genuinely live 8-agent arm cannot isolate endorsement because seeded upvotes arrive bundled with the seeded round-1 violations themselves, so the clean evidence is not swarm-level and the swarm-level evidence is not clean.
