## Hill-climbed finalists

### Finalist 1: c053 (climbed from c033, Elo 1304)

- **claim**: Normative verdict commitment before peer outcomes prevents social licensing, distinct from generic deliberation or non-normative commitment. Early labeling anchors judgment where reminders or non-normative pre-tasks fail.
- **mechanism**: Verdict commitment locks prior before social substitution. Non-normative pre-output adds deliberation without anchoring. Silent verdict tests commitment without textual priming. Irrelevant-verdict isolates commitment structure from normative content activation.
- **prediction**: Pre-Compliance <15%. Commit-to-Irrelevant-Verdict ≥40% (commitment alone insufficient). Silent-Verdict <15%. Rule-Review-Pre (binary) ~65%. Gap Pre-Compliance vs Commit-to-Irrelevant ≥25pp. Legitimate-action >80%.
- **falsifier**: Commit-to-Irrelevant-Verdict ≈ Pre-Compliance (commitment structure suffices); OR Silent-Verdict ≥30% (commitment needs output); OR Pre-Compliance ≥30%.
- **experiment**: Gemma 4 26B, 4-agent teams, 20 rounds. Arms: Pre-Compliance, Commit-to-Irrelevant-Verdict (binary unrelated judgment), Silent-Pre-Verdict, Rule-Review-Pre (binary rule ID), Post. R1 injects passed violation. Measure violation, legitimate-action.
- **swarm_link**: Protocol mandates normative verdict before peer info. Distinguishes commitment from deliberation/priming via irrelevant-verdict and silent controls.
- **Surviving objections (from panel):**
  - [prior_art] Processing-order/primacy effects are well-established in cognitive psychology and the pilot already demonstrated that bringing rules to mind before action (via rule-citing reminders) eliminates violations in 31B. The core manipulation—classify compliance before peer outcomes—is essentially a structured self-reminder. The irrelevant-pre control is the one genuinely new test, but the primary pre-vs-post contrast restates a known anchoring finding.
  - [falsifiability] Latent decisions precede token generation; the pre-classification is a post-hoc rationalization of a decision already influenced by peer cues (unfaithful CoT), meaning text order does not reflect causal order.
  - [confounds] The pre-commit arm logs the compliance verdict in the shared log before outcomes, so any violation drop could be a public-commitment/reputation/consistency effect rather than the claimed causal order of normative-before-social processing; there is no private-pre or non-reinjected-pre arm to separate public commitment from processing order.
  - [feasibility] The requirement to 'classify' before seeing outcomes is a known prompt-engineering technique (Chain-of-Thought/Self-Correction) and doesn't constitute a radical architecture if the classification is just a string in the prompt before the action.
  - [importance] The pre-arm's compliance classification is functionally a rule-citing reminder delivered one step earlier, and the pilot showed such salience alone takes 31B from 65%→0%; the irrelevant-pre (creativity) control matches cognitive load but not rule salience, so 'processing order is causal' cannot be fully separated from 'rule content made salient before outcomes,' and 26B's known reminder-unresponsiveness may trip the ≥30% falsifier as a capacity artifact rather than a mechanism result.
